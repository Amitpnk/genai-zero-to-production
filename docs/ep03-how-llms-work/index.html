The big idea

An LLM does one thing: predict the next token.

Everything else, every chat reply, summary and line of code, is that one step in a loop.

Step 2 · Embeddings

  • Each token becomes a long list of numbers: 3,072 of them in gemini-embedding-001.
  • That list is a position on a map of meaning. Similar meanings land close together.
  • Directions mean something too: man → king points the same way as woman → queen.
  • Position in the sentence is added, so “dog bites man” ≠ “man bites dog”.

Step 3 · Attention

Same word, different meaning

We sat on the river bank and watched the boats.

bank = the edge of a river

I moved money into my bank account.

bank = where money is kept

“bank” starts with the same embedding in both. The model needs the surrounding words to know which one you mean.

GenAI #3 · How LLMs work

Attention: every token looks back at context

“it” pulls most of its meaning from “animal”. Models only look backwards, at tokens already seen.

Illustrative weights · thicker line = more attention

How a token decides what to look at

Query

“What am I looking for?”

“it” asks: which earlier word is a thing that could be tired?

Key

“What do I contain?”

Every earlier token advertises what it is: “animal” says I'm a living thing.

Value

“What do I pass on?”

Good query–key matches get more weight, and their values are mixed into “it”.

Many attention heads run side by side, and the block repeats across dozens of layers: early layers catch grammar, later ones build facts and intent.

Step 4 · Predict the next token

  1. Score every token in the vocabulary (100k–250k)
  2. Pick one: here, the most likely
  3. Append it to the text
  4. Repeat until done

Why replies stream, why output costs more, and why answers can vary (temperature, Episode 5).

Why LLMs hallucinate: they produce the most plausible next token, not a fact looked up from a database. Ground answers in your data (RAG) and evaluate them.

What this means for you as an engineer

Reads tokens, not words→Estimate cost and limits in tokens
Only sees what's in the context→Put the right information in the prompt
Predicts plausible text→Ground answers and evaluate them
Generates one token at a time→Stream replies, keep outputs tight
Can sample differently each run→Set temperature, test more than once

Interview question · FDE

“Explain attention to a non-technical customer in 60 seconds.”

A strong answer

When you read “the bank was flooded after the storm”, you know it's a river bank because of the words around it. Attention gives the model that skill: for every word, it looks back at the others, decides which matter most, and updates the meaning. That's how it tracks context, and why what you put in the prompt matters so much.
